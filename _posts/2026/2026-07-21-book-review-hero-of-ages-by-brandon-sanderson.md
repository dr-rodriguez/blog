I've been re-reading Mistborn after so many years, prompted by recently finishing the first arc of **The Stormlight Archive** and it's been quite the experience. **Hero of Ages** is the third of the original trilogy. Here is the Goodreads blurb:

> *Who is the Hero of Ages?*
> 
> *To end the Final Empire and restore freedom, Vin killed the Lord Ruler. But as a result, the Deepness—the lethal form of the ubiquitous mists—is back, along with increasingly heavy ashfalls and ever more powerful earthquakes. Humanity appears to be doomed.*
> 
> *Having escaped death at the climax of The Well of Ascension only by becoming a Mistborn himself, Emperor Elend Venture hopes to find clues left behind by the Lord Ruler that will allow him to save the world. Vin is consumed with guilt at having been tricked into releasing the mystic force known as Ruin from the Well. Ruin wants to end the world, and its near omniscience and ability to warp reality make stopping it seem impossible. Vin can't even discuss it with Elend lest Ruin learn their plans!*

Read on for my spoiler-free review.

---

## Overall Impression

This is a great book in the original Mistborn series, helping establish it in the broader Cosmere. While the second book in the trilogy suffered a bit in pacing, this one picks it back up and takes it to the finish line. Lots of cool revelations about the nature of the Cosmere and the world of Scadrial in particular. Plus, we get our usual cast of characters who are well-developed at this point with some interesting developments for them.

## Plot

There's a lot going on in the book and it's not as straightforward as the prior novels. There is a big bad out there, which the characters do not fully know about, nor do they have concrete plans to deal with it. They're trying get more information, but this is split over the various groups. You as the reader will know all the information before them so you can make connections that the characters might not. It's still quite bleak, though, and it's hard to imagine how it's going to end until it does.

Despite the seeming meander of the plot threads, it's still fairly fast-paced as there are discrete conflicts in the forms of rebellions being quelled or cities being captured. And all throughout is the mysterious force of Ruin. Towards the latter half of the book, all the stories start to tie together and while several of the characters are in different cities with different conflicts, you see the overall picture form of how it's all fits.

## Characters

We have a familiar set of characters returning from the prior novels. Sazed is depressed and it drags his story down by repeatedly referring to his conflict of faith. He gets much better towards the end, but it's a slog at the start. Spook, on the other hand, has some fascinating developments. Elend is a lot more mature, and Vin is pretty much the same as usual- a straight-to-the-point character, though she has been learning to reason and plan things out a bit better. We also have TenSoon as a kandra viewpoint character, which helps reveal a lot about them. And of course, we have the conflicting powers of Ruin and Preservation which are characters in their own right.

## Setting / World Building

This book, while still being focused on the conflicts of the characters, manages to really open up the setting even more than the prior books. We get more clarity about the metallic arts, including now Hemalurgy. We learn about the nature of Scadrial, the planet where this is all set. And we learn a lot about kandra and koloss and their societies, how they are made, and their strengths and weaknesses. There are a lot of cool revelations all throughout.

This is also where more of the Cosmere is revealed. The first two Mistborn novels focus very heavily on their core plot, even if there are hints to other things. In this novel, we see more clearly the conflict between Ruin and Preservation. We even get a name-drop of "spren" by a Worldhopper (not Hoid), which is fascinating as we wouldn't learn what spren are until The Way of Kings. There's likely tons of little hints like these in Sanderson's books that could be part of future stories we haven't even realized.

## Final Thoughts

This is a very strong finish to the first Mistborn series. It has a slow start, but picks up speed and ends with a satisfying conclusion. There is some interesting character development, but the real shining point is the setting. Not only does this expand what we know about the world, but it also starts setting the stage for the greater universe beyond- the Cosmere.
