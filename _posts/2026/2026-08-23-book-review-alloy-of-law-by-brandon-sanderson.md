Having finished the original Mistborn trilogy re-read with [The Hero of Ages]({% post_url 2026/2026-07-21-book-review-hero-of-ages-by-brandon-sanderson %}) (and the **Mistborn: Secret History** which is too spoiler-filled to review), I've moved on to **The Alloy of Law**, the start of the second Mistborn era, 300 years later. Here is the Goodreads blurb:

> *Three hundred years after the events of the Mistborn trilogy, Scadrial is now on the verge of modernity, with railroads to supplement the canals, electric lighting in the streets and the homes of the wealthy, and the first steel-framed skyscrapers racing for the clouds.*
>
> *Kelsier, Vin, Elend, Sazed, Spook, and the rest are now part of history—or religion. Yet even as science and technology are reaching new heights, the old magics of Allomancy and Feruchemy continue to play a role in this reborn world. Out in the frontier lands known as the Roughs, they are crucial tools for the brave men and women attempting to establish order and justice.*
>
> *One such is Waxillium Ladrian, a rare Twinborn who can Push on metals with his Allomancy and use Feruchemy to become lighter or heavier at will.*
>
> *After twenty years in the Roughs, Wax has been forced by family tragedy to return to the metropolis of Elendel. Now he must reluctantly put away his guns and assume the duties and dignity incumbent upon the head of a noble house. Or so he thinks, until he learns the hard way that the mansions and elegant tree-lined streets of the city can be even more dangerous than the dusty plains of the Roughs.*

Read on for my spoiler-free review.

---

## Overall Impression

This was another good book in the Mistborn universe. The characters are great and the plot fast, but the setting and framing suffer a bit. One is a matter of taste (I'm not the biggest fan of Westerns in terms of setting and stereotypical plots). The other is that it reads more like a standalone than the start of a trilogy. That said, this serves as an introduction to the second Mistborn era with plenty of new things to enjoy.

## Plot

The plot moves quickly and the novel is short enough that the pace never sags. There are lots of action scenes as Wax and Wayne get into fights with criminals, usually outnumbered, and get to do some crazy stunts with Allomantic powers, guns, and time-bending powers to gain the upper hand. This is one of those books that feels like an action movie.

While the story is relatively well-contained, there are hints and teasers to a broader story to be told in the subsequent books. The epilogue, in particular, really sets the stage for more to come.

## Characters

We mainly focus on Waxillium (aka Wax), a noble-turned-lawman, and his sidekick Wayne. They're well established as a dynamic crime-fighting duo with very distinct and recognizable personalities. Wayne is a funny character, a kleptomaniac with a talent for voices. He is the comedic relief to Wax's more somber outlook. Marasi joins in a little later and manages to subvert expectations and be a welcome addition to the team.

## Setting / World Building

Sanderson's settings are always great and this one is no exception. Personal taste aside, we have an interesting setting where Allomancy has continued to be developed alongside technology. Electricity is starting to come online, as well as gunpowder, automobiles, skyscrapers, and more. The growth of magic alongside technology isn't something that is done in a lot of books, making this a fascinating read.

Allomancy itself has some interesting developments as well. There aren't any more Mistborn, instead some people are Mistings, having a single Allomantic power, and some rare ones can be Twinborn, having both Allomancy and Feruchemy powers. You learn very early on how Wax, for example, has steel for Allomancy (allowing him to push on metals) and iron for Feruchemy (allowing him to store weight). This allows for some powerful combinations. I can't wait for the Cosmere RPG setting to see how it describes these, though the Ars Arcanum at the end of the book has some insightful things to say as well.

We also get to see what 300 years of life on Scadrial has done to the story of Mistborn era 1. A lot of the characters and actions are now legends and myths. People know about them, but as religious icons to worship rather than as ordinary people. In terms of the larger Cosmere perspective, this book doesn't offer much except some minor hints from Harmony and teasers of something grander, but I won't spoil that for you.

## Final Thoughts

This is a fun book to kick off Mistborn Era 2. It's 300 years past the original trilogy and we get to see how society, Allomancy, and technology have developed in the intervening years. The Western-style framing isn't my favorite, but there is still much to enjoy, with great characters and a fast-paced action adventure.
