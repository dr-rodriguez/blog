**The Fires of December** comes from a BackerKit project: [Hoid's Storybook Collection](https://www.backerkit.com/c/projects/dragonsteel/hoids-storybook-collection). This is book 3 in Brandon Sanderson's **Hoid's Travails** series and is narrated by Hoid himself. I wasn't interested in the storybooks the campaign focused on, but did want to pick up this Cosmere novel.

Here is the official blurb:

> *In the next Cosmere standalone novel, Hoid has an epic tale to share: In a forgotten land sustained by colorful rivers of demon blood, a young woman named December learns a devastating plague is on its way. But who will believe her? Against impossible odds, she sets sail up the River Violet to warn the king's court, determination—and wisdom beyond her years—burning in her eyes. Along the way she’ll navigate incredulous royal officials, twisted court intrigue, and a roguish count with only her wits and her new friends: a talkative priestess and a fledgling fashion designer.*

Read on for my spoiler-free review.

---

## Overall Impression

This was a fun book. After a rough, oddly toned first chapter, it settles into a decently paced adventure. If you liked the storytelling style of [Tress of the Emerald Sea]({% post_url 2023/2023-01-14-book-review-tress-of-the-emerald-sea-by-brandon-sanderson %}), you'll feel right at home here (if you didn't, be warned: Hoid can and will interrupt the story). And even if you aren't heavily invested in the Cosmere, you'll get something out of it: December's story is interesting on its own, and its themes apply pretty broadly.

## Plot

The first chapter is really weird. It doesn't quite fit the rest of the book in tone or narration style, and it comes off as a very odd, storybook-like tale. It was honestly a bit depressing, and I kept wondering how this was going to become the story. It turns out I wasn't alone: in the postscript, Sanderson acknowledges this was a criticism he received from beta readers. That said, the conclusion of the chapter ties everything together, and once you reach the end of it you're hooked. The subsequent chapters are much better.

The plot is a little slow at the beginning but speeds up quite a bit, especially near the end. December comes from an isolated, simple village. She knows of the king and the like, but she has never visited a big city and doesn't really know what demons are or how people live out there. So as she sets out into the world, we discover it alongside her.

There are also some interesting themes here about making the most of your life, the decisions you make along the way, and how not to let others define who you should be.

## Characters

![](/assets/img/posts/2026/20260925/Fires_of_December_Prerelease_Cover.png){: width="500" .w-50 .right}
December is the main character and the one we primarily follow. She is mysterious and comes across as very passive in that first chapter (which, again, feels off). For the rest of the story, though, she's a bit more proactive. She's a young woman, a fosterling with no family, and her life is a tough one. But once the plot gets going, she heads out into the world alone searching for answers and driven by a strong sense of duty. I enjoyed watching her fight against the boxes people try to put her in.

She's joined by several other characters, including Amaal, the chatty priestess; Tamatha, the fashion designer; Dmitrovik, the footman; Flint, the elderly nobleman; and a mysterious cat. And of course there's Hoid. As the narrator, he frequently jumps in to interject, break the fourth wall, or offer extra information. That extra information can be extremely interesting, especially if you're a Cosmere fan, and it can help relieve tension. But it happens a little too often for my taste, and it can be jarring enough to pull you out of the story.

## Setting / World Building

We're on a new planet we've never seen before: Miral. It feels like a standard fantasy setting with kings and nobles, a hint of magic, and rivers of demon blood. There are little tidbits of Cosmere lore that refer back to the wider universe if you have an eye for that sort of thing. I think we need a dedicated book to help tie all these pieces into a grander narrative. Hoid is clearly looking for something, but all we see are small teases in these short books.

As for the magic (because Sanderson's stories always have magic), it's a little less clear-cut than in some of his other worlds. It centers on demons, which are never fully explained. Some of that is kept vague, probably by design, and some you'll have to read to find out. There is an aspect of bonding the demons, so it harkens back to the spren of [The Stormlight Archive](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/7235533-the-way-of-kings) and some things we saw in [Tress of the Emerald Sea]({% post_url 2023/2023-01-14-book-review-tress-of-the-emerald-sea-by-brandon-sanderson %}). The exact nature of the bond feels different, as we learn in the story.

## Final Thoughts

If you've been reading the Cosmere novels and novellas, you'll enjoy this one. It's more of what you like. Hoid's interjections may appeal to some readers and not others; it's a narrative style not everyone will appreciate. Setting that aside, the plot itself is good, if a bit straightforward. There are lots of little tidbits and mysteries a deep reader can dig into for clues about the larger story of the Cosmere, which I know is something a lot of Sanderson fans are into. This isn't my favorite of the **Hoid's Travails** series (which grew out of the Secret Projects). December is a better character than Tress, but [Yumi and the Nightmare Painter]({% post_url 2023/2023-07-06-book-review-yumi-and-the-nightmare-painter-by-brandon-sanderson %}) is the better story in my opinion.
